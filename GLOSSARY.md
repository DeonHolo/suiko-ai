# Editorial Content Review

A shared vocabulary for the newsroom review scenario. These terms describe the work and its meaning, independently of implementation technology.

## Language

**Story**  
An article, script, or caption being prepared for publication. It has its own revisions and review history.

**Article**  
A written story.

**Content format**:
The form of a story: article, script, or caption.

**Content kind**:
The editorial purpose of a story: news, opinion, or sponsored content. This is distinct from its format.

**Revision**  
A preserved version of a story that can be identified and reviewed independently of later edits.

**Submitted revision**  
The saved copy an editor puts up for review.

**Source note**  
A source link, reporting note, or production note supplied with a revision for the reviewer to inspect.

**Review context**:
Optional briefs, interview excerpts, or production notes supplied to help assess a story. Supplied context remains distinct from material retrieved from a source link.

**Baseline revision**:
The earlier revision used as the reference for a comparison.
_Avoid_: Previous document when the specific reference version is unclear.

**Candidate revision**:
The submitted revision that an AI review examines.

**AI review**  
Advisory analysis of a submitted revision. It can explain changes, suggest metadata, and highlight concerns for the editor.
_Avoid_: AI approval, automated legal clearance.

**Editorial policy**:
The publication's approved standards for preparing and reviewing its content. A policy can apply across many different stories.

**Proposed editorial rule**:
A suggested check derived from the publication's policy that is awaiting an editor's approval.

**Rule template**:
A reusable suggested editorial rule that an editor can approve, edit, remove, or replace. A template becomes an active company rule only after approval.

**Editorial rule**  
An editor-approved standing instruction the publication uses to check drafts where the rule applies.

**Rule applicability**:
The content formats, content kinds, and circumstances in which an editorial rule should be assessed.

**Editorial checklist**  
The set of editorial rules supplied for a review.

**Editorial rule version**  
The saved wording and applicability of a rule at the time of a review.

**Rule approval**:
A designated editor's recorded acceptance of the exact wording and applicability of an editorial rule version.

**Finding**  
A concern about a specific passage or note in a submitted revision that asks for human judgment under an editorial rule.
_Also called_ a potential checklist conflict.
_Avoid_: Proven violation, automatic rejection.

**Needs confirmation**:
A review result for an applicable rule that cannot be decided from the material available. It identifies what an editor needs to establish.

**Evidence reference**  
An identifiable passage in a submitted revision, supplied context, company policy or retrieved source that supports a review observation. Its material and version are distinguishable.

**Retrieved source**:
Material actually accessed from a supplied source link for a review. A supplied link alone does not establish that the source was read.

**Source snapshot**:
The preserved material from a retrieved source that was available to a particular review, with its source identity and retrieval details.

**Source support**:
Whether material actually read supports a particular claim in a story. Support from a source does not by itself establish that the source or claim is truthful.

**Metadata suggestion**:
A proposed story classification or description awaiting a human decision.

**Review currentness**:
Whether a saved review applies to the revision and editorial rules being considered.

**Review coverage**:
The requested analysis areas and checklist criteria that were assessed, distinguished from work that remains incomplete or was not requested.

**Human decision**:
An accountable editorial or legal review action made by an authorized person, separate from AI analysis.

**Legal clearance**  
A legal reviewer's decision that the specific submitted revision can proceed to editor sign-off.

**Editor sign-off**  
An editor's decision that the specific submitted revision is ready for publication.
